2. Terms of use

Community Defense Map is for protecting each other: it maps police activity and surveillance. Reports are visible to everyone, and each person is responsible for what they post.

Report situations, never people.
  • Not allowed: names, nicknames, physical descriptions, badge numbers, license plates, or anything else that identifies a person, whether a protester or an officer.
  • Not allowed: insults, threats, calls for violence or hatred, discriminatory statements.
  • Not allowed: false information and deliberately misleading reports.

Content that breaks these rules is removed without notice. During an attack (a flood of fake reports), reporting can be paused or reports erased.

Crowd-sourced information, not the truth. Reports come from participants and are not verified unless marked “Verified by” an organization. They can be wrong, incomplete or out of date. Use your own judgment, stay with your group, and in a medical emergency or serious danger call 911.

The Rights tab gives general information about U.S. law. It is not legal advice: if you are arrested, talk to a lawyer.

Community Defense Map was designed to collect as little data as possible. No account, no cookies, no ads, no analytics. The publisher (see above) is responsible for this data.

Why this data is processed: to provide an information and safety service and to protect it against abuse. Data is not sold or shared with third parties, except the technical providers named above (host, OpenStreetMap for the map, browsers’ notification services).

What this protection does not cover: the host, or anyone who takes control of the server, can technically read what it holds at that moment (active reports, current fingerprints, notification subscriptions, organizer keys in hashed form). The emergency wipe erases all of that and destroys the keys, but a software wipe does not reach the host’s backups or snapshots, copies already made elsewhere, or physical traces on the disk.

In a sensitive situation, you can use Community Defense Map through a VPN or the Tor Browser.

You may have rights under the privacy laws of your state. Because Community Defense Map collects minimal data and cannot identify you, most requests cannot be fulfilled by linking data to you. You can write to the address below with questions.
